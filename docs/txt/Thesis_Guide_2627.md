The Master’s thesis is the conclusion of your studies at ESCP Business
School. This research project is an in-depth analysis of a precise topic, based
on an innovative and insightful analysis. The goal is to synthesize current
knowledge and to create new knowledge through the collection of original
data and through a personal analysis. This project demands high standards
and creativity.

This work must be carried out on a long-term basis. It takes time to
specify a problem statement or research question, evaluate existing literature
on a topic, collect and analyse field data, and finally to compare this with
existing knowledge, which your data will confirm or invalidate.

Start looking into research topics now, which are suggested by professors, in
order to submit your thesis topic. These topics offer you lines of enquiry in
which you will have to choose a research subject and phrase it in your own
words.

Phrasing a research question or problem statement is already an
exercise in and of itself. Begin as soon as possible!

provides you with methodology support; he/she advises you during your

    1.1    Choosing a research topic

The choice is based on your main interests. Once the area of research is identified, phrasing and
defining the scope of research will be carried out with the Scientific supervisor.

What is a good topic?
  – an original topic, which has never been studied in that perspective before,
  – a relevant topic, which would lead to answer management questions or which is linked to
       the business world,
  – a realistic topic, which can reasonably be dealt with within the time constraints and for
       which field data are available. Being overly ambitious with your research may lead to rough
       guesses, which must be avoided.


1.2 Defining the research question

The main question must be chosen beforehand.

                                   Example:
Subject:
Innovation in family-run businesses

Main question:
Are family-run businesses more innovative than other companies?

This is a broad theme which can be handled in many ways and you will not be able to do a full
analysis of each perspective. Hence choices must be made. The problem statement takes that into
account, as the problem statement is the perspective from which you will analyse the question, like
a photographer who chooses an angle to take a picture.

    2. Research
    2.1     Literature review

    Your subject must be original. Nevertheless, there will be topics similar to yours that have
already been published and discussed. You must immerse yourself in this literature. Note that
books directly related to your research topic seldom exist. If this was the case and if everything had
already been written, your subject would hold no interest. Yet you may often find subjects which
are close to your own topic or which deal with concepts you introduce in your problem statement.
For example, many works and papers have been published on innovation, which will help you give
your own definition of innovation and make your initial choices. For example, you may choose to
research product or service innovation or innovation in organisations.

You will find research papers more easily than books, and more frequently in Anglo-Saxon journals
than in French ones.

Each time you read a paper or a book, write down its exact references and sentences you find
relevant for your research (do not forget to write the page number as well!) and sum up the key
points. These files will be very useful when you are in the writing stage of your thesis.

You must establish a reference point and guiding theme. The first reference is the most difficult to
find. Afterwards everything becomes easier; you will find a bibliography in each and every research
paper. Finding the first paper will lead you to other articles and so on.

    Do not mix up literature review and bibliography:

 The literature review consists of listing and analysing previous research published on the subject
and its related topics. It is not just about reading and summarising these works. You have to point
out their results in relation to your problem statement. At this point, it is advisable to meet experts
in your research area, so that they can confirm whether your problem statement is relevant.

 The literature review and meetings with experts will help you clarify your research question, and
even discover a new question. At the end of this stage, you will be able to draw up hypotheses.
Hypotheses stand for possible answers to your problem statement; the aim of your research is to
validate them following your field survey or further analysis of existing data.

2.2         Drawing up a research method

     “ La connaissance est un processus itératif entre le réel (que l’on étudie) et la représentation de ce réel “dans
                                                                                                        1
     la tête” (concret pensé, construction théorique, recomposition idéale du concret perçu ou vécu). ”
Howard S. Becker2 agrees and explains that research is a dialogue between the idea and the
evidence.

This part will help you answer the following questions:
     Which information must be collected to answer the problem statement and confirm the
      hypotheses?
     How to collect data (survey, case study…)?
     Which model will allow measuring the data and how can it be sure that the data are reliable?
     Which field to choose for your survey (industry, companies, consumers…)?

It would take too much time to introduce all the different research methods here. You must start
by consulting one of the works mentioned above. You will then specify an adequate research
method with your Scientific supervisor.


2.3       Collecting, analysing and reading into data

       This stage is the core of your research. Once you have delved into the field, you will apply
the previously specified methods, to give a fully justified answer, validating or invalidating your
hypotheses. It can be split up into 3 steps:

         Carrying out the field research
          Do not forget that you usually need some time before getting an appointment with a
          company manager, in general at least 2 weeks. Besides, field research is not always relevant;
          some topics will require more of a data analysis.

         Analysing and reading into the results
          Facts do not speak for themselves; you need to deliver an interpretation of them. This step
          will allow you to give some (often incomplete) answers to your problem statement.

         Being critical on the methods and research limits

    3. Writing

                  3.1      Problem statement


        La problématique, c’est “l’ensemble construit autour d’une question principale, des hypothèses de recherche et
        des lignes d’analyse qui permettront de traiter le sujet choisi”
                       (Cf. Michel Beaud, L’art de la thèse, Guides Repères, La Découverte, 1994, p. 32).

This first part consists of a definition of the subject, a literature review, a bibliography, the problem
statement, the possible research hypothesis, the research method and plan of action. This stage
ends in February and takes place in the middle of your project.


                  3.2      Final paper


                  Writing a paper is always more time-consuming than expected!

The first version is seldom the right one. In most cases, it lacks introductions and transitions.
Subsequently, you should plan to write these transitions to link the different parts together. Then
you have to delete unnecessary or redundant parts, find the adequate balance and develop points
which are not clear enough. Finally, you must add a table of contents with page numbering, a
bibliography and page numbering of the appendices.

Do not forget that you also need time to do the report, graph, page numbering and bibliography
formatting, which is also demanding and time consuming.


                     Do not forget to do regularly back-up copies of your work.

  3.4       Size of the paper


 60 pages minimum are required (without appendix). Some Master theses may also have many

    3.5      Example of typical structure


          Introduction: research objectives, subject’s relevance, agenda

      1. Presenting the research area thanks to a critical literature review, problem statement

      2. Explaining the research method and analysing their limits.

Diagrams, tables and illustrations

Font, line space and margin

The paper ends with a declaration that the work has indeed been carried out by the student and

This declaration must also mention that the master thesis is an original work that has never been
submitted during studies prior to ESCP Business School. If it is a paper which has been written as
part of parallel studies, it must be stated.
This declaration is to be downloaded from your thesis space (Documents to download >
Affidavit).

Plagiarism detection
All Master theses are analysed to detect plagiarism.


 3.5.3   Bibliography


 Any writer must be quoted. Do not forget: copyright laws exist.

You commit plagiarism and/or fraud when you copy or paraphrase a part of a text without
indicating its reference or a part of another student’s work and making it your own.

(Plagiarism or fraud leads to a disciplinary committee – see ESCP Business School Rules and
Policies, Section 2 – Article 3: Plagiarism).

Please apply the following standards to your Master thesis:

   In the text, any borrowing (quote, graph or table) must be referred to its author like this: name,
   date.
   Example: “As Falk (1994) pointed out, the debate...”

   In the bibliography, the author must also be found:

                                                16
    Falk, Pasi (1994), The consuming body, Sage, London.

    The title of the books must be written in italic.
    The title of articles must be written between quotation marks and the name of the publication

Your choice of a timeslot is final. It is thus important, before selecting an appointment, to confirm

The oral defence is one hour long.

The Master thesis’ defence takes place in front of a jury composed of at least the jury’s chairman
(your Scientific supervisor), and a professional chosen by the student. The Scientific supervisor has
to validate the professional guest chosen by the student.

It is divided into two parts:

    1. A 20-minute presentation: Students present their work with the support of the usual IT
       presentation tools. Students are reminded that they must comply with the time
       constraints of the oral defence. It must focus on the main issues of the project, pointing
       out the method and the results of their research. It aims at making sure the students
       master their subject, controlling their presentation and persuasion skills. They must prove
       they have understood the stakes of the subject and they are able to communicate a clear
       message;
    2. A debate with the jury: The choice of the professional guest is decisive. Students must
       make sure he/she will be there because his/her role in the debate is complementary to
       the professor’s. The professional is either a representative of the business world
       (minimum 2 years of professional experience) or an academic expert on the subject.
       His/her role consists in looking at your work with an outside eye and in bringing his/her
       operational knowledge of the subject. The name of the professional must be submitted to
       the Scientific supervisor for validation. A student or a member of your family is not
       allowed to take part of the jury as the professional member. Students must check the
       professional’s profile with the professor and make sure this profile is relevant to the
       subject and that the discussion would help underlying strength of the work. The debate is
       an opportunity to highlight some aspects which might otherwise remain unaddressed and

         elements the paper, since this work being now over.

Some advice:

    -    Do not exceed the time limit which is set. You should practice in order to manage your
         time properly on the day of your defence.
    -    A good presentation links the major conclusions of your research with new perspectives

    -    Consider that the jury read your paper carefully. Hence you do not need to sum up what

    -    The presentation allows you to make incidental remarks about some research elements

    -    Do not hesitate to be critical of your research without actually depreciating it. Step back

         learned during the project.
    -    Your tone, volume and level of formality must be appropriate. You will not deliver a

7.1 Article 3 Plagiarism

Article 3.1 Principle

Plagiarism consists of borrowing, imitating, or copying another individual's work while taking credit
for it, particularly without citing a source. The source can be the work of other individuals enrolled
in training its own work already submitted, as well as published material.

Using ideas is not reprehensible in itself. What is objectionable, on the other hand, is borrowing
the form, the expression, or the structure of the ideas' presentation.

Moreover, using a text without giving a source can be considered counterfeiting, which is against
the law.


Article 3.2 Exceptions

For all graded ("marked") work, the individual enrolled in training must use his or her own words,
sketches and drawings, templates and models, ideas, and arguments. The work submitted is
presumed to be the individual's. However, if the individual wishes to base his or her work on what
he or she has read or heard, the individual must indicate the verifiable source(s), as described below:

Quoting:
Quoting an excerpt from a book, article, or another individual's work is authorized as long as:
1. the excerpts are presented between quotation marks,
2. the sources are given in a format that allows them to be verified, for example, by indicating the
reference via footnote or cross-reference or in another fashion,
3. the excerpt represents a reasonable portion of the work submitted.

Paraphrasing:
Paraphrasing, which consists of using another individual's ideas with the one's own words, is
authorized as long as the use of this technique is clearly mentioned (e.g., "Dupont (1986)
demonstrates that …").
In the same fashion as quoting, the reference of the main work must be given in a footnote or in
the bibliography.

7.2 Code of Conduct for Using Large language models (LLMs) and Other AI Tools to
Work on any assignment or thesis

                                                  20
At ESCP Business School, we recognize the value of technology in aiding academic research and
writing. However, we also value academic integrity and honesty. As such, we have created this code
of conduct to ensure that students use LLMs and other AI tools appropriately and ethically when
working on their assignment or thesis.
It is acceptable to use LLMs and other AI tools to:
                   Help you improve the clarity, coherence, and quality of your writing.
                   Develop counter-arguments or ideas to help you expand your perspective and
                    develop your critical thinking.
                   Identify relevant and appropriate papers to read and cite (check references
                    before incorporating them into your theses!).
                   Review and criticize your work, so that you may further improve it.
If you choose to use LLM and other AI tools, you are responsible for ensuring that the information
that you use from LLMs and AI is accurate and relevant.
It is not acceptable to:
                   Use a prompt and copy-paste the output into your paper word by word without
                    proper attribution or paraphrasing. Doing so constitutes plagiarism and violates
                    academic integrity.
                   Use LLMs and other AI tools to generate entire sections of your paper without
                    engaging in critical thinking or conducting original research. Doing so violates
                    academic integrity and misrepresents your own knowledge and skills.
                   Use LLMs and other AI tools to fabricate or manipulate data, including
                    literature, interviews, survey responses, case studies, etc. for your study. All your
                    data must be properly collected as indicated and discussed with your professor.
                    If it is part of your work to generate data from LLMs and other AI tools, please
                    specify it in your work.
Professional tools to detect plagiarism will examine assignments and more specifically the thesis.
Unacceptable use of LLM and other AI tools will result in a failure of the course or thesis and
students, may be considered as fraud and lead to a Disciplinary Committee, which sanctions can
go up to exclusion from the school.
We expect all students to use LLMs and other AI tools ethically and responsibly, and to adhere to
the principles of academic integrity and honesty at all times. If you have any questions or concerns
about how to use LLMs and other AI tools appropriately, please consult your professors.
By following these guidelines, students can ensure that their use of LLMs and other AI tools is
ethical, responsible, and contributes to the advancement of knowledge and scholarship. The
student has full responsibility for the content of his or her work.
