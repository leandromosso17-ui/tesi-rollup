                 Summary Sheet - Thesis Methodology
        This document summarises the methodological and operational elements of the guide in order to
support students throughout the thesis development process, from formulating the topic to the defence.

 Core idea: a thesis is not simply a compilation of readings. It is an individual, original, reasoned and
 critical research project that starts from a clear question, draws on the academic literature, uses an
 appropriate method, analyses reliable data and produces a personal contribution.



1. Important methodological points for formulating and writing a
research thesis
1.1 Choosing a relevant research topic
●   Originality: the topic must offer a new angle or address a question that has not yet been widely studied
    from this perspective.
●   Relevance: it must make it possible to answer a question related to management, organisations or the
    business world.
●   Pragmatism: it must be feasible within the allotted time, with accessible data. An overly ambitious
    topic often leads to approximate claims.
●   Precision: a general theme must be transformed into a genuine research topic. For example, “disruptive
    innovation” is too broad, whereas “the role of market research in the development of disruptive
    innovations” is a more precisely targeted question.

1.2 Formulating a research question
●   The main question must be defined early: it guides the entire research process, the reading, the method
    and the structure.
●   The research problem corresponds to the chosen angle of analysis: it does not simply name a theme; it
    constructs a research perspective.
●   A good topic avoids purely descriptive formulations. It must call for analysis, explanation, comparison
    or empirical validation.
●   The research problem must be capable of generating hypotheses or lines of analysis that will then be
    tested against fieldwork or data.

1.3 Defining the concepts
●   Before beginning, the concepts used must be defined precisely in order to avoid any ambiguity.
●   The definitions must be supported by the literature and consistent with the research question.
●   This stage makes it possible to delimit the topic: for example, by specifying what is meant by
    “innovation”, “family business”, “performance”, “external growth”, etc.
1.4 Building a critical literature review
●   The literature review is not limited to summarising articles: it analyses existing work in relation to the
    research problem.
●   It is used to identify established findings, debates, limitations, blind spots and the concepts required for
    the research.
●   Notes must be taken on each reading: exact references, key ideas, useful quotations, page numbers and
    links to the research problem.
●   The bibliographies of the first articles identified provide an entry point to other relevant references.
●   Priority should be given to academic databases, scientific journals, ESCP libraries, Google Scholar and
    specialised databases.

1.5 Defining a coherent research method
●   The method must derive from the research question and hypotheses, not the other way around.
●   You must determine what information is needed, how to collect it, in what field setting and with which
    tools.
●   The method may include a survey, interviews, a case study, analysis of existing data, a comparison of
    cases or another appropriate approach.
●   The reliability of the data must be discussed: source, collection method, possible biases, sample
    limitations and validity of the measurements.
●   Methodological limitations must not be concealed: they must be identified and discussed critically.

1.6 Analysing data rigorously
●   Facts do not speak for themselves: they must be interpreted in light of the research problem and the
    literature.
●   The results must be presented clearly, with reasoned and critical analysis. The analysis must show how
    the data confirm, qualify or invalidate the hypotheses.
●   The empirical results must be studied both for what they are and with sufficient distance to identify
    possible generalisations or conceptualisations that are not excessive and that contribute to
    understanding the topic addressed.
●   Where relevant, the thesis should, where possible, propose recommendations or courses of action.

1.7 Paying attention to academic writing and structure
●   Writing takes longer than expected: several versions should be planned.
●   A good thesis includes introductions, transitions and interim conclusions that guide the reader.
●   The structure must be logical: it must advance the reasoning, not merely juxtapose information.
●   Repetitions, unnecessary passages and imbalances between sections must be corrected during
    proofreading.
●   The style must be fluent, precise, formal and free from errors.
  ●   Clarity is the priority in this type of writing.

  3. Practical Checklists
  Before starting to write
  ●   The topic is sufficiently precise and has been approved by the supervisor.
  ●   The research problem is clearly formulated.
  ●   The central concepts are defined.
  ●   The main academic references have been identified.
  ●   The method is consistent with the research question.
  ●   The necessary data can be accessed within the deadlines.

  During the writing process
  ●   Each section addresses the research problem.
  ●   The arguments are supported by the literature or the data.
  ●   The transitions make the reasoning easy to follow.
  ●   Quotations, tables, charts and data are correctly referenced.
  ●   Methodological limitations are explicitly discussed.
  ●   Managerial implications are clearly formulated.

  Before final submission
  ●   The table of contents is up to date.
  ●   Pages, figures, tables and appendices are numbered.
  ●   The bibliography follows the required format.
  ●   The thesis contains the required abstracts and keywords.
●   The non-plagiarism declaration has been added.
●   A recent backup copy of the file is available.
●   The text has been proofread to correct spelling, grammar, repetition and inconsistencies.


4. Academic Integrity and Use of AI Tools

Please read the AI code of conduct available in the Student Handbook here.

●   Any borrowed quotation, idea, table or chart must be clearly referenced, even when it is paraphrased.
●   Plagiarism includes copying, unreferenced paraphrasing and reusing previous work without
    acknowledgement.
●   They must not be used to produce entire sections without personal critical work, or to fabricate data,
    references, interviews or results.
●   The student remains fully responsible for the accuracy, originality and integrity of the submitted
    content.

●   The use of artificial intelligence must be discussed with the thesis supervisor during supervision
    meetings in order to verify compliance with ethical rules.


5. Assessment Criteria


●   Understanding of the topic and clear identification of the problem.
●   Relevant, valid and academic references.
●   An appropriate methodology that is correctly applied.
●   Relevant and reliable data.
●   Rigorous and critical interpretation of the results.
●   Identification of stakeholders and impacts.
●   Innovative and well-discussed proposals or recommendations.
●   Relevant structure and reasoning.
●   Fluent, accurate writing and a faithful summary in the abstract.
●   An oral presentation consistent with the thesis and the ability to answer questions rigorously.

How the Registration Campaign Works
        The calendars are provided at the end of this document and by email at the beginning of the
registration campaign. You can find all useful documents in your thesis area throughout the year here.

The registration campaign is organised in two waves. Each wave is divided into three stages:

        Stage 1 - Topic Submission - Students submit a summary of their topic, consisting of a title and
an abstract, to one professor only via the Web Aurion platform.

        Stage 2 - Topic Selection - Professors accept or reject the topics submitted by students. In the
event of rejection, they must select a reason from the available options.

        Stage 3 - Publication of Results - Students will receive an email informing them whether the
professor has accepted their topic.

       The deadlines must be strictly observed, as the platform closes at the end of each stage. At
the end of each wave, students who have not been accepted will automatically be registered for the
next wave.

        Final Phase - At the end of these two waves, students who have not been registered with a
professor will automatically be integrated into the final phase. Students must submit a new application,
this time without assigning it to a specific professor. Professors who are still available will review the
platform and select student topics. Once a topic has been selected by a professor, registration will be
completed automatically.

        Where prior agreement has been reached with a professor, the student may include the
professor’s name in the topic title to prevent another professor from selecting the topic.

The registration in a resit depends on meeting the deadlines indicated (see “RULES” and “Calendar”
documents), not on the wave after which you are accepted by a professor, therefore you will not be
registered in a resit if you are not accepted during the wave 1 or 2 or during the final phase.
How Should You Choose a Topic?
We strongly recommend applying the methodological advice set out above.

You can find thesis topics in your Thesis area here.

You may choose one of the topics on this list or propose your own topic. If several students choose the
same topic, the professor will decide either to retain the same topic or to propose an alternative in the
same field.


What Should My Research Project Presentation Contain?
         You were asked to prepare, during the summer, a one- to three-page presentation of your research
project, accompanied by a bibliography. The presentation of your research project must explain your
topic, including:

       a title;
       context;
       the research question;
       the proposed method;
       a bibliography (you will be required to submit a literature review in January, so remember to
        take notes as you read).

You may also explain why you chose this topic, if this information is relevant to your research.

You must demonstrate your ability to answer a question by following a scientific method, rather than
simply presenting your motivation for working on the topic.

Your document does not need to be perfect—and probably will not be, since this is the beginning of your
work. It should serve as a basis for discussion with your thesis supervisor, so that they can guide you in
your research.

Based on this presentation, you will need to write a summary of your research project of no more than
400 words in order to submit your application on the platform.

Do not neglect the research project presentation stage. A clear presentation will enable you to
write a good summary for the application process. It will also prevent you from wasting time
during the first months of your research.
How Do I Know Which Thesis Supervisor to Ask?
If you have chosen your own topic, you must look for a faculty member whose research covers the same
field or fields.

You may choose your thesis supervisor based on the topics they have proposed or their current research,
in particular by consulting the Internet:

    ● in the list of 2026-2027 topics, available in your Thesis area here ;
    ● in the list of ESCP faculty members:
      https://www.escp.eu/faculty-research/faculty-members

Please note that, in the latter case, not all professors are available to supervise a thesis this year.

In both cases, you must check the professor’s availability by consulting the list available on the platform.
It is updated when the platform is open for student applications. The calendar is provided at the end of
this document.

You may ask to be supervised by a professor who is not based at one of the campuses where you will
study during the year.

Example: if I study in Paris and London, I may ask to be supervised by a professor based in Turin. If
they accept, I will be affiliated with the Turin campus for my thesis.

All thesis defences may be organised online.
